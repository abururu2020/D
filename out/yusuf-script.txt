Allah calls it the best of stories. His whole story is told in a single surah, from beginning to end. This is the story of Yusuf, peace be upon him, told the way the Quran tells it.

It begins with a boy and a dream. Yusuf said to his father, the prophet Yaqub, peace be upon him: O my father, I saw eleven stars and the sun and the moon. I saw them prostrating to me.

Yaqub knew what the dream meant, and he knew his sons. He said: O my son, do not tell your vision to your brothers, or they will plot against you. Satan is a clear enemy to man.

And he told him something more. Your Lord will choose you, and teach you the meaning of events, and complete His favor on you and on the family of Yaqub.

But the brothers already felt it. Yusuf and his brother are more beloved to our father than we are, they said, and we are a strong group.

So they made a plan. Kill Yusuf, or throw him out to some far land, and your father's attention will be yours alone.

One of them said: Do not kill Yusuf. Throw him into the bottom of the well, and some travelers will pick him up.

They went to their father. Why do you not trust us with Yusuf? Send him with us tomorrow, to eat and to play. We will guard him.

Yaqub said: It saddens me that you would take him, and I fear a wolf would eat him while you are not watching.

They took him, and they agreed to put him into the bottom of the well. And in that moment, Allah inspired the boy: one day you will tell them about this, and they will not know who you are.

That night they came back to their father, weeping. We went racing, they said, and left Yusuf with our things, and a wolf ate him.

They brought his shirt, stained with false blood. Yaqub said: No, your souls have tempted you into something. So patience is most fitting. And Allah is the one whose help is sought.

At the well, a caravan stopped for water. Their water drawer let down his bucket, and called out: Good news! Here is a boy.

They hid him as goods to trade. And they sold him for a small price, a few silver coins.

The man who bought him in Egypt was a great official, called al-Aziz in the Quran. He said to his wife: Make his stay comfortable. Perhaps he will benefit us, or we will take him as a son.

And so Allah settled Yusuf in the land. When he reached maturity, Allah gave him wisdom and knowledge.

Then came his first great test. The wife of al-Aziz, in whose house he lived, tried to seduce him. She closed the doors.

Yusuf said: I seek refuge in Allah. He is my master, and he has treated me well. The wrongdoers never succeed.

Allah turned evil away from him. He was one of Allah's chosen servants.

Yusuf ran for the door, and she ran after him. She tore his shirt from the back, and at the door stood her husband.

She accused Yusuf at once. But a witness from her own family said: If his shirt is torn from the front, she is telling the truth. If it is torn from the back, she is lying.

The shirt was torn from the back. Al-Aziz said: Yusuf, let this pass. And to his wife: ask forgiveness for your sin.

But the story spread through the city. So she invited the women who spoke about her, and gave each of them a knife. When Yusuf came out before them, they were so amazed that they cut their own hands. This is no human being, they said. This is a noble angel.

She threatened him with prison. Yusuf prayed: My Lord, prison is dearer to me than what they call me to. And his Lord answered him.

So Yusuf, innocent, was sent to prison.

Two young men entered the prison with him. One dreamed he was pressing wine. The other dreamed he carried bread on his head, and birds were eating from it.

Before he explained their dreams, Yusuf called them to Allah. O my two companions of prison, are many separate lords better, or Allah, the One, the Prevailing?

Then he told them. One of you will serve wine to his master again. The other will be crucified, and birds will eat from his head.

He said to the one who would go free: Mention me to your master. But Satan made him forget, and Yusuf stayed in prison for several years.

Then the king had a dream. Seven fat cows eaten by seven lean ones, and seven green ears of grain, and others dry.

His advisers said: These are mixed-up dreams. And at last, the man from the prison remembered Yusuf.

Yusuf explained it. Seven years you will farm as usual. Leave what you harvest in its ears, except a little to eat. Then seven hard years will come, and eat what you stored. Then a year of relief will come.

The king said: Bring him to me. But Yusuf would not leave prison until his name was cleared. Ask about the women who cut their hands, he said.

The women said: We know no evil of him. And the wife of al-Aziz said: Now the truth is clear. It was I who tried to seduce him, and he is truthful.

The king said: Today you are established with us, and trusted. Yusuf said: Put me in charge of the storehouses of the land. I am a knowing guardian.

The boy from the well now held the food of Egypt.

The hard years came, and reached Yusuf's family too. His brothers came to Egypt for grain, and entered upon him. He knew them. They did not know him.

He gave them full measure, and said: Next time, bring me your brother from your father. Without him, there is no grain for you.

Yaqub said: Should I trust you with him, as I trusted you with his brother before? But Allah is the best guardian, and He is the most merciful of the merciful.

They swore by Allah to bring him back. In Egypt, Yusuf took his brother aside, and said: I am your brother. Do not grieve over what they used to do.

Then Yusuf placed the king's measuring cup in his brother's bag. It was found there, and by the brothers' own judgment, his brother stayed in Egypt.

The eldest refused to go home. The others returned and told their father. Yaqub said again: Patience is most fitting. Perhaps Allah will bring them all back to me.

He turned away and said: Oh, my sorrow for Yusuf. His eyes turned white with grief.

He said: I only complain of my grief and sorrow to Allah. My sons, go and search for Yusuf and his brother, and never despair of relief from Allah.

The brothers came to Egypt again, poor and humbled. Yusuf asked them: Do you know what you did to Yusuf and his brother?

They said: Are you really Yusuf? He said: I am Yusuf, and this is my brother. Allah has been gracious to us.

They said: Allah has truly preferred you over us, and we were sinners. Yusuf said: No blame on you today. Allah will forgive you, and He is the most merciful of the merciful.

He said: Take my shirt, and cast it over my father's face. He will see again.

As the caravan left Egypt, Yaqub said: I can smell Yusuf. And when the shirt was cast over his face, his sight came back.

The whole family came to Egypt. Yusuf raised his parents up onto the throne, and they all bowed before him, as the stars, the sun and the moon had in his dream.

Yusuf said: O my father, this is the meaning of my dream from long ago. My Lord has made it come true.

And he prayed: Creator of the heavens and the earth, You are my protector in this world and the next. Let me die as one who submits to You, and join me with the righteous.

The well, the false accusation, the prison, the long years of grief. In every one of them, Allah's plan was quietly at work. Patience, trust, and forgiveness carried the day.

In their stories there is a lesson for people of understanding. That is the story of Yusuf.
